A bifana is a Portuguese pork sandwich. Thin pork simmered in garlic, wine and beer, then piled into bread that soaks up the sauce. This is the PrivacyNotes founder's own recipe and his favourite meal, written down at last so it does not get lost.

![A bifana on a crusty roll, with the sauce pooling on the plate.](/onboarding/bifana.webp)

<p style="text-align: center"><strong>One pot. About half an hour. Feeds four hungry people.</strong></p>

## What you need

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Pork loin | 1 kg |
| Onions | 2 medium |
| Garlic | 6 cloves |
| White wine | 250 ml |
| Beer | 330 ml |
| Tomato paste | 1 tbsp |
| Paprika | 1 tbsp |
| Salt | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | 1 tsp |
| MSG | 1 tsp |
| Butter | 1 tbsp |
| Bay leaves | 3 |
| Chilli | as much as you dare |

Fat for the pan: neutral oil, olive oil, lard or beef tallow. Whichever you have available.

This recipe forgives a lot. Skip what you do not have. Swap in what you like better. It still works.

## Before you start

- [ ] Slice the pork thin
- [ ] Slice the onions
- [ ] Press the garlic and set it aside. It goes in near the end
- [ ] Open the beer and the wine

> [!tip] How thin is thin?
> Eyeball it. Some people want it flaky thin, others want more texture. There is no rule, and it is your pot. Flaky thin is the recommendation here: 3 to 5 mm. More surface area, more flavor.

## How to cook it

1. Heat the fat in a wide pan until it is **very hot**.
2. Add the pork in one layer. Sear it hard, but stop before it browns all over.
3. Add the onions, the tomato paste, the paprika, the salt, the pepper and the MSG. Cook it all together for about two minutes.
4. Pour in the **beer** and drop in the bay leaves. Scrape the brown bits off the bottom of the pan as it bubbles.
5. Turn the heat down low. Simmer for **10 minutes**.
6. Add the **wine**, the pressed garlic and the butter. Simmer for **10 minutes** more.
7. Taste it. Fix the salt, the pepper and the chilli.

> [!tip] Why the garlic goes in late
> Garlic that simmers for half an hour tastes flat. Pressed and added near the end, it keeps its edge. That is the one step people get wrong.

The sauce should have a small kick. Not hot. Just enough to notice.

## On the plate

<p style="text-align: center"><span style="font-family: ui-serif, Georgia, Cambria, serif">Crusty bread. A baguette. A soft bun. All three are correct.</span></p>

Rice works. Pasta works. So does a bowl and a fork, standing at the counter, at eleven at night.

> [!info] Without pork, or without alcohol
> Chicken, beef and lamb all take the same treatment. To leave the alcohol out, use stock in place of the beer and the wine, and add a splash of vinegar.
